Section 1 of 6

Summary

  1. LinkedIn is our biggest channel, with 3,385 followers. In the last 8 weeks we published 7 own posts with a median of 6 reactions per post, and nothing has gone out since 31 August. The six direct competitors sit at a median of 12 reactions per post.
  2. Not one of our updates comes from a named person. At ChannelMojo the whole feed consists of posts by people, with a median of 50 reactions on 2,094 followers. At team5pm the posts by people reach 35 and 71 reactions, while the company posts stay between 6 and 27.
  3. Only one of our posts got more than 10 reactions: the Pacvue day at the office, with 32. It is also the only post with video, a partner and tagged colleagues.
  4. The LinkedIn page tells two different stories. English visitors read a D2C text, Dutch visitors an older Amazon text, and neither text carries the line the website uses: “The D2C Performance Agency”.
  5. Instagram is split over two accounts. The website only links to @10xcrew_europe, which is not visible without login. The account that actually works, @10xcrew_agency with 71 followers and 75 posts, is not linked from anywhere.
  6. YouTube has 23 subscribers, 28 videos and 14,996 views. The last 10 uploads are all Shorts. A joke about birth years has 2,775 views, the Pacvue expert Short has 56 and the Amefa workshop 11.
  7. The competitors come across as thought leaders through four things: named people, an own event series, own research data, and client cases with numbers. Seven of the ten agencies we compared run a newsletter. We run none.
  8. We already own the material and hardly show it: the chair of an expert group, a jury seat, bol Gold Partner status and five named client cases with results. The proposal is to fix the channel identity first, then let named experts and client cases carry LinkedIn, and re-use that content on Instagram and YouTube.

All figures were read from the public pages on Monday 14 September 2026 between 13:47 and 14:30, without logging in. “Last 8 weeks” runs from Monday 20 July to Monday 14 September 2026. Every number has a source file with the raw page and the exact time of the request.

LinkedIn: three observations

Rhythm breaks

Seven own posts in eight weeks comes down to 0.9 a week. The week from 7 September was empty, and on the measurement date the page had been silent for fourteen days. The internal norm is one to two posts a week.

People beat the company voice, even on our own page

Insight and document posts reach 4 to 6 reactions, and the post with the CCO on the jury reached 8. The Pacvue training video with twelve tagged colleagues reached 32. The post of 17 August (“7 in 10 shoppers abandon their cart”) links to the contact page instead of a blog post.

The page tells an old story, twice

The tagline still reads “Creating online solutions for growth.” English visitors get an About text on D2C strategy, Dutch visitors get “Kickstart and grow your Amazon and online direct sales.” Growth of +2 followers in eight days is below what a page of this size gains without doing anything.

Instagram: three observations

Two handles, one dead link

The header, the footer and /visit-our-channels/ all link to @10xcrew_europe. Six requests in two rounds returned the same error page as a made-up account name, so whether the account still exists cannot be settled from outside.

The working account is hidden

@10xcrew_agency has 71 followers, follows 156 accounts and holds 75 posts. No page on 10xcrew.com links to it.

Content still unjudged

The bio, the date of the last post, likes and comments are only visible with a logged-in account. That means the internal engagement benchmark of 0.48% cannot be applied yet.

YouTube: three observations

Low rhythm, Shorts only

Two uploads in eight weeks, on 23 July and 31 August. The last ten uploads are all Shorts of 13 to 56 seconds.

Views follow humour, not expertise

“what year did you born” has 2,775 views, “Social commerce” 1,932 and “office gossip” 780. The Pacvue expert Short has 56 views and the Amefa onboarding workshop 11. The median number of likes is 4, and none of the ten has a comment.

Presentation errors

The titles are in lower case, the most viewed title contains a language error, a client name is written without a capital (“workshop with amefa”), hashtags are duplicated and one hashtag has a typo (#10XCRE). Who watches, and from where, only YouTube Studio can tell.

Five patterns

Named people outperform the company voice

ChannelMojo’s feed consists entirely of reposts of its own people: 7 in 8 weeks, 9 visible, with a median of 50 reactions over those nine and a top post of 155, on 2,094 followers. At team5pm, the posts by two named leaders (71 and 35 reactions) beat every company post, which stay between 6 and 27.

Follower count does not predict activity

e-tailize has 19,929 followers and last posted on 19 June. DEPT, with 199,164 followers, gets 56 reactions on a regular post. Reach is earned per post.

Own events are the strongest thought leadership format

Five of the ten agencies show an event series on their own site: FolloTalks, GoSpooky Invites, Fightclub Ringside, DEPT Future Club and Adwise N3YID. ChannelMojo’s Marketplace Meetups only live on LinkedIn. None of these events takes place in Eindhoven or Brainport.

Proof beats claims

Brightways’ Maesy case (+33% revenue year on year, TACOS under 9%) reached 25 reactions, against 3 to 5 for its other posts. GoSpooky’s Stichting Reclame Code case puts its numbers in the copy itself: 217K+ landing page views and a CTR of 6.9% against 3.3%. team5pm, DEPT and Adwise publish their own reports and whitepapers.

Instagram and YouTube follow the business model

The social commerce agencies keep large Instagram accounts (GoSpooky 343 posts, Fightclub 456, team5pm 661). The marketplace specialists ChannelMojo and Brightways have no confirmed account on either platform. YouTube is small for everyone. team5pm, the largest, has 220 subscribers, but it puts a client case with results in every video description.

What this means: five opportunities

These are recommendations drawn from the measurements above, not facts. The persona letters refer to the five buyer personas in our internal brief: A Marketplace Manager, B Head of E-commerce, C EMEA E-commerce Director, D Founder/CEO of a D2C scale-up, E Brand or marketing lead for social commerce.

1 · Fix channel identity before adding volume

What
Choose one official Instagram handle and link it from the website. Give LinkedIn one About text in one language, with the tagline “The D2C Performance Agency”. Correct the YouTube titles and hashtags, and repair the “[formidable id=3]” form on /visit-our-channels/.
Audience
All personas, at the moment they check the agency before making contact.
Effect
No dead links and no outdated positioning when a prospect clicks through from the site or from YouTube.
Measure
A monthly link check in which every channel link on 10xcrew.com resolves, and consultation requests from social traffic in GA4 needs login.

2 · Let named experts post, and repost them from the company page

What
The CCO (chair of the ShoppingTomorrow marketplace expert group and jury member of the bol Retail Media Awards 2026) and the Head of Social Commerce post from their own profiles. The company page reposts them.
Audience
A, B and C on LinkedIn.
Effect
Reactions above our company page median of 6, following the pattern at ChannelMojo and team5pm (median 50 and 53 on posts by people).
Measure
Reactions and comments per person post, set against the median of 6, and LinkedIn follower growth per month needs login.

3 · Turn client cases with numbers into a recurring LinkedIn document post

What
One case a month as a document post, taken from the five named cases on our site: Stanley 1913 (+169% visibility, 18× revenue growth), Bowers & Wilkins, Signify (Light Gallery), Body & Fit and Prénatal. None of them appeared in the last ten LinkedIn updates. A short version with the expert on camera runs as an Instagram reel and a YouTube Short.
Audience
B and D.
Effect
More reactions than our insight posts (4 to 6), as Brightways saw with its KPI case (25 against 3 to 5). Document posts are the best performing LinkedIn format in the benchmark we use (7.00% engagement, Socialinsider).
Measure
Reactions per case post, plus the engagement rate per format in Page Analytics and the YouTube traffic sources for the Short needs login.

4 · Start a small, recurring peer event in Eindhoven

What
A quarterly session for marketplace and e-commerce leads of Brabant brands, together with a partner. Announce it and post a recap with named people. The Pacvue day (32 reactions, our best post) shows that this format already works for us.
Audience
A and B.
Effect
A reason for people outside 10XCREW to mention and repost us. None of the ten agencies compared has an event in Eindhoven or Brainport.
Measure
Registrations, reactions on the announcement and the recap, and the number of external posts that mention 10XCREW.

5 · Publish one own dataset per quarter, and start a newsletter to carry it

What
A short benchmark that connects marketplaces and social commerce, built on the expert group’s knowledge or on our own measurements, published on the blog together with a LinkedIn document post. Seven of the ten competitors run a newsletter. We have 87 blog posts this year and no way to send them to anyone.
Audience
B, C and trade journalists.
Effect
A number that others cite, as team5pm does with its CMO survey, and a mailing list we own instead of an algorithm we rent.
Measure
External mentions and reposts, newsletter sign-ups, and referral traffic to the dataset page in GA4 needs login.
